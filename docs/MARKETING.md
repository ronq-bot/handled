# Handled — voice, campaigns, and the jokes that do the selling

## The one-line pitch

**Never forget her birthday ever again.** Set every date once. We ship the gift on the day.

## Voice

Confident, dry, self-aware. The joke is always *at the user's expense, on his behalf* — he's in
on it. Three rules:

1. **He's the punchline, she never is.** "You forgot" is funny. Anything at her expense reads
   bitter and kills the purchase. This is a hard line, not a preference.
2. **Short sentences. No exclamation marks.** The humor comes from flat delivery, not volume.
3. **Every joke ends in a benefit.** "You will still forget it" is only funny because the next
   sentence is "so we won't."

**Never do:** nagging-wife tropes, ball-and-chain jokes, "happy wife happy life," or anything
implying she's a problem to be managed. It's lazy, it's the reason competitors read as cheap,
and half the actual buyers are women buying it *for* the man in their life.

## Campaign concepts

### 1. "The Five Stages of Remembering Too Late"
Already built into the landing page. Denial → Bargaining → Gas Station → The Look → The Doghouse.
Works as a carousel, a :30 spot, and six billboards. The Gas Station panel is the shareable one.

### 2. "Doghouse Risk Meter"
Our best growth asset because it's a real feature, not a gimmick. A free public quiz — enter your
dates, get a risk score and a shareable card. The score is only fixable by signing up. Runs the
same `dogHouseRisk()` code as the dashboard.

### 3. "Certified Thoughtful"
Lean into the badge. A man screenshots his 0/100 risk score into the group chat; his friends'
scores are catastrophic. Peer pressure is the entire funnel.

### 4. Seasonal panic ads
Two weeks before Valentine's, Mother's Day, and Christmas, target the men frantically searching
"same day delivery." Copy: *"Too late this year. Not next year."* — the only ad that converts a
failure into a subscription.

### 5. "Office Villain"
Bouquet lands on a desk at 10 AM. Coworkers stare. Cut to their husbands' phones lighting up.
Tagline: *"Send it to her work. Ruin someone else's marriage."* — the highest-recall concept, and
the one that needs the most careful execution to stay warm rather than smug.

## Channel notes

| Channel | Angle |
|---|---|
| Paid social (Meta / TikTok) | Stages of Forgetting carousel; Office Villain video. Target men 28–55, married/engaged. |
| Search | Buy the panic terms: "same day flower delivery," "last minute anniversary gift," "forgot my wife's birthday." Land them on a page that says *too late this year, fixed forever.* |
| Podcast reads | Ideal format — the host reads it as a confession. Sports, comedy, business. Script it as a personal admission, not ad copy. |
| Reddit / forums | r/relationship_advice adjacent, but organically. The Risk Meter quiz travels; the product pitch does not. |
| Referral | "Get a friend out of the doghouse." A month free per side. Men will absolutely send this to friends as an insult. |
| Retention email | Ship-day email: *"Her flowers arrive today. Act natural."* This is the single most important email in the product — it's the moment the subscription justifies itself. |

## Positioning against the alternatives

| They offer | We offer |
|---|---|
| A reminder ("her birthday is in 3 days!") | The gift already ordered and en route |
| A monthly flower subscription | The *right* gift on the *specific* dates that matter |
| A gift card | Something that arrives in a box with her name on it |
| Remembering at 11 PM the night before | Deciding it in March, calmly, at a desk |

The competitive line: **reminder apps create a to-do. We complete it.**

## Naming and identity

**Handled** — the pun is the point: *alphabetical order* / *placing an order* / the man
getting his life in order. Ironic and self-deprecating, not chest-thumping. Keep the "α" mark
and the flame-orange accent; the dark UI reads as a tool, which is what makes automating
sentiment feel competent rather than cold.

---

## Lines the product itself should say

Microcopy is the marketing. What's shipped in the app today:

- Empty dashboard: *"A calendar with nothing on it is just a poster."*
- No people added: *"There's nobody in here."*
- Weekend shift notice: *"The date falls on a weekend and nobody is at the office — delivering the Friday before."*
- No payment method: *"Everything above is scheduled but nothing can actually ship. This is the single most common way people end up in the doghouse with a full dashboard."*
- Delivered event: *"Delivered. You're a hero and you did nothing."*
- Card message field: *"Write it now, while you have thoughts."*

## Guardrails

- Testimonials must be labeled as illustrative until they're real. The landing page already does.
- No fake urgency, countdown timers, or invented scarcity.
- Don't promise same-day nationwide before the courier integration exists (see
  [VENDORS.md](VENDORS.md), Phase 3).
- Never imply we'll email or contact the recipient. The entire value is that she doesn't know.
